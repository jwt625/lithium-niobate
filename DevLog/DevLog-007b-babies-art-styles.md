# "For babies" books: one art style per chapter (2026-10-28)

Precedent: advanced-packaging-atlas DevLog-015 + `media/hybrid-bonding-short/STYLE.md`. Scene size there sets the effort
scale: **S** = style kit ~100-200 lines (Swiss 102, Mondrian 132, Bauhaus 189), **M** ~300-400 (cyanotype 292,
pointillism 318, riso 339), **L** ~600-760 (ukiyo-e 584, Art Nouveau 601, red chalk 606, engraving 764). A book = one
style kit plus 10-14 page compositions, so a book costs roughly kit + 0.3-0.5 kit for the pages. Carry over the
precedent's rules: deterministic `render(t)`, seeded static textures (no per-frame noise), no images, no tracing.

Legal baseline (applies to every row):
- US: works published 1930 or earlier are PD as of 2026-01-01 (95-year term) — https://web.law.duke.edu/cspd/publicdomainday/2026/
- EU: author's life + 70 years, running to 31 Dec — https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2006/116/oj ; faithful reproductions of PD visual art get no new copyright (Art. 14) — https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2019/790/oj
- US copyright does not cover "any idea, procedure, process, system, method of operation" — https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/102 . That covers methods (stitching, carving, isometric projection, pictogram counting) but **not** specific compositions or characters. So: use the method, invent our own compositions, and avoid signature motifs.
- Avoid entirely: Miffy/Dick Bruna (d. 2017-02-16; Mercis won a copyright and trademark case against Sanrio's "Cathy", Amsterdam 2010-11-02 — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miffy), Eric Carle collage look (d. 2021 — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Carle), Escher (d. 1972; the M.C. Escher Company enforces rights — https://mcescher.com), IKEA manual mascot, LEGO studs, TfL roundel, Gerd Arntz glyphs (see ch08).
- Death years below link to Wikipedia or the museum. I re-checked these in this session: Bayeux, scratchboard, Lissitzky, Arntz, Miffy, jianzhi (UNESCO page), Bruegel (KHM page), Duke 2026. The rest are standard dates that I did not re-fetch.

## Cover: Chinese paper-cut (jianzhi), red on cream
- **Why:** LN is point group 3m. Fold a paper disc in half, then into thirds, to get a 60 deg wedge; cut it and unfold. The creases become 3 mirror lines 60 deg apart, which generate the 3-fold axis: the result is exactly 3m (a snowflake needs 30 deg wedges, 6mm). The fold-and-unfold is the cover animation and also a symmetry lesson. Red on cream gives high contrast.
- **Refs/PD:** folk method, no author; on UNESCO's Representative List since 2009 — https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/chinese-paper-cut-00219 . Risk: none.
- **Recipe:** SVG. Draw one 60 deg wedge path: seeded crescents ("moon" cuts), sawtooth edges, teardrops, plus an Nb/Li/O motif. Mirror it to 2 copies, then rotate by 120 deg to 6 (`<use transform>`). Fill-rule evenodd for holes. Paper red `#C0262D` on `#F3ECDD`, with a lifted-paper shadow `rgba(0,0,0,.18)` offset 3 px. Animation: the wedge sits folded, unfolds into 2 halves, then into the full 3m figure (rotate/scaleX about the crease lines, ease-out 0.8 s). **Effort S.**
- **Alternates:** (a) a sampler grid with one tile per chapter style (a preview of the series); (b) Scherenschnitte silhouette in the manner of H.C. Andersen's papercuts (d. 1875 — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Christian_Andersen).

## 01 Crystal: counted cross-stitch on Aida cloth
- **Why:** the cloth grid is a lattice and every X is one cell. In cross-stitch the top leg of every X must slant the same way. That rule is the polarization: "/" on top = +P domain, "\" on top = -P. A domain wall is a backstitch line. Poling is re-stitching a region so its top legs flip. Li/Nb ions are French knots. Black/red/white on cream suits babies.
- **Refs/PD:** 17th-19th c. needlework samplers, anonymous — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sampler_(needlework) . Risk: none. It is close to the video's 8-bit pixel look. What sets it apart: thread texture, and the stitch direction carries the physics.
- **Recipe:** Canvas2D. Static Aida ground `#F2ECDF` with a weave of alternating 2-tone cells and hole dots `#D6CBB6` at the grid nodes. Each stitch is 2 round-cap strokes, width 0.32 cell, thread colours `#1A1A1A` / `#C62828` / `#FFFFFF`, plus a 1 px lighter highlight stroke and seeded +/-0.04 cell jitter. Backstitch is a single dark stroke along grid edges. Animation: an electrode bar sweeps and the stitches re-cross one row at a time, so the domain wall advances. **Effort M.**
- **Alternates:** (a) Roman mosaic (opus tessellatum): tesserae are unit cells and the andamento flow lines are domain walls; (b) pieced patchwork quilt: half-square triangles point the dipole, and poling flips blocks. For (b), use generic 19th c. blocks and avoid the Gee's Bend quilters (living artists).

## 02 Light: medieval stained glass
- **Why:** light passing through crystal is the whole chapter, and stained glass is lit from behind. Lead cames are waveguides with sharp black edges. Birefringence becomes one beam entering a pane and two leaving, with hatch directions marking the polarizations. SHG becomes a dark pane (1064 nm, drawn with an IR hatch) feeding a 532 nm green pane. Keep wavelengths honest: no "red in, green out". A comb is a row of equal narrow panes.
- **Refs/PD:** Chartres Cathedral glass, mostly early 13th c. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chartres_Cathedral ; Sainte-Chapelle, consecrated 1248 — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sainte-Chapelle . PD everywhere. Do not trace photographs of real windows.
- **Recipe:** Canvas2D. Panes are authored polygons, or seeded Voronoi cells clipped to shapes. Lead: `#1C1C1C` stroke 8-12 px, round joins, 1 px `#555` highlight. Glass: cobalt `#1F4FA8`, ruby `#B3122E`, emerald `#1E8A4C`, amber `#E2A21B`, violet `#5B2A86`, clear `#DCE5E7`. Each pane gets a radial gradient for backlight, static seeded fbm streaks at 8 % for antique glass, and a few bubbles. Animation: a glow (`globalCompositeOperation='lighter'`) travels pane by pane along a came channel. **Effort M.**
- **Alternates:** (a) Analytic Cubism, multiple simultaneous views for the double image, after Juan Gris (d. 1927, PD in the EU, and in the US for works published by 1930 — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Gris). Avoid Picasso (d. 1973) and Braque (d. 1963), both still protected in the EU. (b) Orphism, Robert Delaunay's simultaneous-contrast discs (1912-13) for spectra and combs (d. 1941, EU PD since 2012 — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Delaunay). Avoid Sonia Delaunay (d. 1979).

## 03 Sound: black-line woodcut / linocut relief print
- **Why:** the carving method is the wave. Gouge strokes follow wavefronts. For a SAW the stroke density thins with depth as exp(-z/lambda), so the Rayleigh wave's confinement to about one wavelength is visible in the cut. BAW is flat parallel cuts. The IDT is a carved black comb. Black + one red is high contrast.
- **Refs/PD:** Hokusai, Great Wave, c. 1831 (d. 1849 — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Great_Wave_off_Kanagawa); Felix Vallotton's 1890s woodcuts (d. 1925 — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%A9lix_Vallotton); Munch, *The Scream* lithograph 1895, where wavy bands carry sound (d. 1944, EU PD since 2015 — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edvard_Munch). All PD. **Partial reuse:** this is the same relief-print family as the video's colour ukiyo-e, but the look is different (black-line Western relief).
- **Recipe:** Canvas2D. Paper `#EFE6D2` with static seeded tooth (multiply 6 %). Ink `#141414`, spot `#C23B22`. Each gouge stroke is a tapered filled quad (pointed ends, max width 3-7 px) laid along iso-lines of u(x,z)=A cos(kx-wt) e^(-z/lambda). Edges roughened 2 px with seeded noise. Static ink-starve speckle mask. In solid blacks, wood grain as sin(f*(y+noise)) rings. Animation: shift the phase wt but quantize to 10 fps, cycling 3 pre-seeded inkings so the print "boils". **Effort M.**
- **Alternates:** (a) ukiyo-e Great Wave in colour (reuse; strongest single SAW image); (b) Chladni sand figures (Chladni 1787, d. 1827 — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernst_Chladni): particles settle on the nodal lines of computed plate modes, which shows BAW resonator modes. This is physics-as-method.

## 04 Quirks: crayon scratch-art (scraperboard)
- **Why:** photorefraction is light writing into the crystal and staying written. Scratch-art works the same way: each stroke permanently reveals colour under black. Optical damage is a scribble you cannot undo. Pyroelectric charge is scratched sparks when the page warms. DC drift is a pointer re-scratched a little further each page. Li out-diffusion is a halo of scratched dots spreading from the edge. Bright on black suits babies.
- **Refs/PD:** modern scraperboard "originated in the 19th century in Britain and France" — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scratchboard . It is a school/craft method with no author. Risk: none.
- **Recipe:** Canvas2D, two layers. The underlayer is wobbly crayon bands `#E8352E #F28C28 #F6D03C #3BB273 #2F6FDE #8E44AD` with waxy streak noise. The top is `#111111` with a faint sheen. Scratch with `destination-out` strokes 3-6 px, drawn twice with jitter for ragged edges, plus seeded burr flecks. Animation: reveal the stroke path prefix by arc length (the light beam drawing the grating). The grating persists after the beam leaves. **Effort S.**
- **Alternates:** (a) generic Surrealist dream picture: hard cast shadows, sky inside objects, objects with agency. Use Grandville, *Un Autre Monde* 1844 (d. 1847, PD — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._J._Grandville) as the precedent. Avoid Magritte motifs (d. 1967, EU-protected through 2037 — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ren%C3%A9_Magritte) and Dali's melting clocks (d. 1989 — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvador_Dal%C3%AD). (b) paper marbling (suminagashi/ebru): tine and drop displacement fields, for diffusion and self-written patterns.

## 05 Making: wordless instruction-manual isometric line art
- **Why:** fabrication is a numbered procedure. Exploded isometric views show the stack (wafer, bonded film, electrodes) and the order of steps, and callouts handle the scale jumps (boule, wafer, waveguide).
- **Refs/PD:** isometric projection formalized by William Farish, "On Isometrical Perspective", 1822 (d. 1837 — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Farish_(chemist)); step-by-step craft plates in Diderot-d'Alembert's *Encyclopedie* (plates 1762-1772 — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encyclop%C3%A9die). Both PD. Generic isometric step diagrams carry low risk. Do not imitate IKEA's mascot figure, blue/yellow palette, or product-name jokes, or LEGO bricks.
- **Recipe:** SVG. Projection x'=(x-y)cos30, y'=(x+y)sin30-z. Painter's order for hidden faces. Uniform 3 px `#111` lines on `#FFFFFF`. Top faces `#D9D9D9`, sides white. One accent `#E4572E` for the active agent (heat, ions, plasma, field). Numbered step discs, chunky arrows, magnifier callout circles. Animation: parts slide along iso axes into place; in Czochralski the seed rotates and rises while the boule radius r(z,t) grows. **Effort M.**
- **Alternates:** (a) blueprint/cyanotype (reuse of the video's l6b; Herschel 1842 — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyanotype); (b) felt quiet-book applique: blanket-stitched layers stack the way bonded thin films do, and it is a tactile baby genre.

## 06 Devices: Constructivist picture book after El Lissitzky
- **Why:** devices are constructions, and the chapter is a cast of machines built from elementary shapes. Lissitzky made a children's book from exactly this: *About Two Squares*, a red and a black square arriving on Earth. Red/black/cream matches the newborn high-contrast palette. Mapping: the modulator is a red square (light) passing between black electrode bars and tilting when they thicken; the comb is equal thin red bars; quantum pairs are one square splitting in two; the RF filter is a black comb; the laser is a red wedge.
- **Refs/PD:** *About Two Squares*, Berlin 1922 — https://www.moma.org/collection/works/11113 ; Lissitzky d. 1941 (EU PD since 2012; US PD as published 1922) — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Lissitzky . Risk: none. This is adjacent to the video's Bauhaus. The difference is diagonal dynamic composition versus the Bauhaus orthogonal planes.
- **Recipe:** SVG/Canvas2D with flat primitives only (rect, circle, wedge, thin bar), set on 15-45 deg diagonals, no outlines. Palette `#D7262B` `#141414` `#F2EBDD`, optional `#8C8C8C`. Letterpress feel: static 4 % ink-density noise and 1 px misregistration of the red plate. Animation: shapes fly in along diagonals and lock with an ease-out (the precedent's rising-disc pattern). **Effort S.**
- **Alternates:** (a) paper quilling (Regency craft): coils as ring resonators, spiral delay lines, combs; (b) Ben-Day halftone screen print (Ben-Day process, 1879 — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Day_process): avoid Lichtenstein compositions and speech-balloon pastiche (d. 1997 — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roy_Lichtenstein).

## 07 History: Bayeux-style embroidered strip
- **Why:** a single continuous narrative strip is a timeline. Pages become windows panning along one 1801-2026 band, and the border registers repeat small crystals and waves.
- **Refs/PD:** made in England, probably the 1070s; about 68 m x 0.5 m embroidery, wool on linen; stem-stitch outlines, laid-and-couched fills, 10 dye colours — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayeux_Tapestry . PD everywhere.
- **Recipe:** one long Canvas2D strip with page windows over it. Linen `#E8DEC7` with static weave crosshatch. Wool palette (10): `#A8432F #C8A23C #6F8B5A #2F4A4F #33456E #D8C39A #3B2A20 #8A5A3C #B9B07A #5E2F2A`. Laid work is parallel thick strokes in each region, with perpendicular couching ticks every ~8 px. Stem stitch is short overlapping dashes along outlines. Figures are flat, in profile, Bayeux proportions. Animation: pan, with each era's figures stitched in as an outline-then-fill stroke reveal. **Effort L** (figure drawing dominates). Every event shown must come from ch07 `data/events/history.yaml`.
- **Alternates:** (a) Chinese handscroll, Zhang Zeduan, *Along the River During the Qingming Festival*, 12th c. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Along_the_River_During_the_Qingming_Festival ; (b) early Sunday comic strip after Winsor McCay (*Little Nemo* from 1905; d. 1934, PD in the US and EU — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winsor_McCay).

## 08 Industry: Isotype counting book
- **Why:** Isotype shows quantity by repeating one pictogram, never by scaling it. That is a counting book, the native baby genre. It also carries "how many wafers / who makes what / where LN is in your phone" honestly, rock to chip in rows. Any number shown must come from ch08 data with its evidence grade, rounded to whole icons.
- **Refs/PD:** Otto Neurath (d. 1945, EU PD since 2016), e.g. *Gesellschaft und Wirtschaft* 1930 — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isotype_(picture_language) . **Risk:** the Gerd Arntz estate (Arntz d. 1988) asserts copyright in the Vienna-era pictograms, bought in 2011 — https://hyphenpress.co.uk/?p=1313 . So draw our own glyphs. The counting grammar is a method (17 USC 102(b)). Risk is low if no Arntz glyph is copied.
- **Recipe:** SVG `<symbol>` + `<use>` grids. Flat stroke-less silhouettes on a 24-unit grid. `#1E1E1E #C8372D #2D5DA8 #D9A441` on `#F4F0E6`. One glyph = one fixed unit; partial units are cut vertically. Animation: icons pop in one at a time (count 1...10), and rows slide left to right along the supply chain. **Effort S.**
- **Alternates:** (a) Bruegel bird's-eye busy panorama, the whole chain in one landscape (*Children's Games* 1560; d. 1569 — https://www.khm.at/en/objectdb/detail/321/); (b) octilinear transit-map diagram (rock, powder, boule, wafer, chip, phone). Avoid the TfL roundel and Beck's specific map.

## Constraints
- **Theme:** the book page is always paper (or scratch-black in ch04); it ignores the theme. Each style is a fixed palette, and inverting stained glass or paper-cut breaks it. Only the surround (letterbox, controls, page counter) follows dark/light. In dark theme, don't dim the page itself: the art keeps its colours and the surround carries the darkness.
- **Corners:** keep UI chrome sharp: the page frame, buttons, progress bar, text panel. The art is exempt, because round lead joins, French knots, paper-cut curves and quilling coils are intrinsic to the methods. The page rectangle stays square-cornered, so the site rule holds where it applies.
- **Text:** use **one typeface across all books** for reading aloud and consistent rhythm, set in a fixed band that never overlaps the art, at >= 4.5:1 contrast on its own paper or black panel. Candidates (SIL OFL): Andika (SIL, built for beginning readers, single-story a/g) or Atkinson Hyperlegible. Keep the site's Newsreader if continuity matters more. Per-style lettering only for each book's title page, via a hook like the precedent's `text(g, word, ...)`. Letter sizes, line counts and positions must stay the same across pages, so text never shifts the layout (layout-shift rule).
