A crystal that steers light with electricity

Meet lithium niobate.

A clear, slightly golden crystal of lithium, niobium, and oxygen. Put a voltage across it and light inside it changes, billions of times a second. That simple trick carries much of the world's internet traffic, and is now heading into AI data centers, quantum labs, and your phone's radio.

Here is the real thing, simulated live: a thin slice of the crystal seen between crossed polarizers. Drag the voltage and watch the dark cross split. That is the Pockels effect.
